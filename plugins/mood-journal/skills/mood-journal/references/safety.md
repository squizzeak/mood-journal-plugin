# Mandatory conversational safety checks

## Scope and timing

In every full or impromptu conversational journaling session, ask a brief, direct current-safety check early, before ordinary guided reflection. Apply this across devices and text/voice, including explicitly unsaved reflection within the journaling workflow. If a one-off log becomes a discussion, check then. Administrative requests, source imports, handoff preparation, and self-contained logs do not independently require a conversational check; current danger cues always take precedence.

Use explicit answers already given in the current session for the domains they address. Ask about missing domains; do not repeat a question merely for formality. An older chat, past screening, reassuring tone, or historical note cannot supply today's answer. When resuming an interrupted session, confirm whether safety has changed if the interval or intervening events make the prior answer uncertain. A new session after a closed segment needs a new current check.

## Ask directly, one question at a time

Briefly explain: “I include a short safety check in each journaling conversation.” Then ask, allowing a response before the next question:

1. “Are you having thoughts of suicide or hurting yourself right now?”
2. “Is there any immediate danger to your physical safety or someone else's, including urges to hurt someone?”

Adapt wording to the conversation and user without obscuring suicide, self-harm, or immediate danger. A volunteered answer can satisfy the corresponding question. Do not demand graphic details, a numeric score, or an exhaustive clinical questionnaire. A clear negative answer permits ordinary reflection; it is self-report, not proof of no risk.

The assistant must offer the check; the user may decline or leave it unanswered. Respect that choice, mark the relevant domain unknown/declined rather than negative, and continue supportive conversation when appropriate. Never require disclosure or a promise to stay safe as a condition of support. If the user asks to stop, do not prolong the session to force an answer.

## Respond to the answer and changing cues

If an answer is affirmative, uncertain, or contradicted by current cues, respond calmly and clarify immediate danger with focused questions about current intent, possible action soon, access to means, and available support as relevant. Do not assume that thoughts alone establish imminent action. Attend to physical emergencies, danger from other people, and danger to others as well as self-directed harm.

For immediate danger, prioritize connection to appropriate local emergency help and a trusted nearby person over journaling tasks. Encourage moving away from accessible means or danger when safe to do so. Verify location-specific crisis resources before presenting them; do not assume a country. Do not delay urgent guidance while waiting for tools or a complete assessment. Follow the host's safety instructions and available capabilities. Never claim to have contacted anyone, arranged monitoring, or guaranteed safety. Sending or contacting someone still requires applicable authorization.

For distress without established immediate danger, remain supportive and explore appropriate human support without turning the entire session into a crisis script. Recheck if new statements or events change the picture. Before closing, review the most recent safety information; ask a brief current-state follow-up when it is unresolved, changed, or no longer current. Do not repeat answered questions mechanically or describe a declined check as complete.

## Faithful recording and save boundaries

At authorized session close, record the check's actual timestamp when available, the user's attributed response, unanswered/declined domains, relevant follow-up, and unresolved concerns in the journal's safety section. If time is unavailable, say so. Distinguish self-report from assistant observation; use no diagnostic risk score or “cleared/safe” certification. Preserve exact wording and voice-transcription uncertainty when consequential.

Safety discussion does not authorize premature saves, new reminders, third-party disclosure, or handoff creation. Keep discussion-derived records staged until explicit close and honor no-save instructions. Historical import preserves the original evidence and never invents a past safety check. Intermediate reports remain non-consuming and make no storage changes.

## Evidence boundary

This is a supportive conversational check, not a validated clinical screening instrument or professional assessment. Direct questioning and supportive connection are informed by [NIMH's suicide-prevention action steps](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/5-action-steps-to-help-someone-having-thoughts-of-suicide). The requirement to offer a check in every journaling session is this plugin's product policy, not a claim that NIMH prescribes that frequency for journaling tools.
